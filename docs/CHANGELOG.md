## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **The network composed on the catchment graph in a fraction of a second** (new `POST /api/delineate-network/preview`; ADR-081, plan `docs/plans/2026-11-11-network-preview.md`; new `core/network_preview.py`, `api/endpoints/network.py`, `models/schemas.py`, `core/catchment_graph.py`, `core/network_tree.py`, `core/network_delineation.py`): the request of `POST /api/delineate-network`, answered without reading the rasters of A's catchment. The points snap and the tree is built as in the full run, so the points, their `at_mouth`, the ids of the subcatchments, reaches and junctions and the errors of the composition (400, 404) are those the full run gives; each subcatchment has its area exact to the cell (the full run's), its outline from the sub-catchment polygons (the largest sub-catchment of any threshold that it holds whole, united on a grid of 10 m; `outline_approximate` where a point splits a sub-catchment of 1 ha, marked `inside_unit`), each reach the difference of the flow distances of its ends (the full run's) and the stream lines between them; `over_scs_cn_limit` above 250 km². No lock and no 422; other failures are 500 `network_preview_failed`. On production's data: 0.08 s for the Kamiennik, 0.18 s for the Kaczawa node, 0.37 s for the whole area of 792 km² (the full run: 3 s, 6.5 s, 17 s). `trace_main_channel` reads the upstream neighbours from the arrays of the graph (the same main channel, about nine times faster).
- **(i) of the network's elements and reaches** (`js/info-texts.js`, `docs/INFO_TEXTS_SOURCES.md`; ADR-080): in the mode „Sieć zlewni” „Zlewnia górna”, „Dopływ” and „Przyrzecze” (the kinds list and the header of a selected element) and „Długość odcinka”, „Spadek odcinka” and „Długość odcinka wzdłuż cieków BDOT10k” (the table of a selected reach) explain what Hydrograf measures and what Hydrolog's Muskingum–Cunge does with it, with their sources (HEC-HMS, NEH 630-17, Ponce) in the register of claims (87 entries).
- **Mode „Sieć zlewni”: a network of subcatchments at control points on the map, handed to Hydrolog** (ADR-080, plan `docs/plans/2026-11-10-network-delineation.md` stage S4; new `js/network.js`, `js/app.js`, `js/api.js`, `js/handoff.js`, `js/map.js`, `index.html`, `css/app.css`, guide and presentation): a button in the mode bar after „Wybierz zlewnię” (five modes, also on phones). The first click places the closing section A, the next ones P1, P2, … (at most 30): numbered markers that drag with the mouse or a finger, listed in the panel „Sieć zlewni” with a name field and „Usuń”; the panel names the network the points snap to („Punkty dociągane do cieków progu 10 ha”, the threshold of „Linie spływu”). „Wyznacz sieć” sends them to `POST /api/delineate-network` with the loading state of the panel; every marker then moves to the stream cell it snapped to („przy ujściu dopływu” on a point moved to the junction below it) and the map shows the elements filled by kind (zlewnia górna, dopływ, przyrzecze) with their ids, the reaches as lines with theirs, the junctions as dots, A's boundary and the legend „Sieć zlewni”. The panel gives A's area and the counts, the check „Suma elementów = zlewnia A” with both areas, the notices (subcatchments above 250 km², reaches with the minimum slope) and the tree from A upward (the branches of each junction one step in, the main river down the list), each element with its area and CN, each reach with its length and slope. A row of the tree, or the label of an element or a reach on the map (a click elsewhere places a point), shows its parameters: an element's in the sections of „Wybierz zlewnię” with their (i), without the hietogram and the hydrograph; a reach's length, slope and its source (NMT, or the minimum), the NMT at its ends and the BDOT10k check length. „Przekazanie do Hydrologa” hands off the network: „Kopiuj do Hydrologa” and „Plik przekazania” (`<subject>.handoff.json`) with „Zawiera sieć: … bez opadu … i bez przekrojów odcinków …”, opened in Hydrolog's „Sieć i trasowanie” with „Wczytaj z Hydrografa”. A change of the points or of the threshold marks the result outdated (its hand-off waits for the next run); an error shows in the panel's error box and marks the points it names (`details.point_id`, `details.point_ids`). Leaving the mode takes the network, its markers and its section away and coming back shows them; the watershed of „Wybierz zlewnię” leaves the map in the mode and returns after it, with its parameters and hydrograph. „Wyczyść” and the bin of the panel remove the points and the result; Esc only hides the panel.
- **A network of subcatchments, reaches and junctions at control points, handed to Hydrolog in one piece** (new `POST /api/delineate-network`; ADR-080, plan `docs/plans/2026-11-10-network-delineation.md` stage S3; new `api/endpoints/network.py`, `core/handoff.py`, `core/messages.py`, `models/schemas.py`, `core/network_tree.py`): the request names a closing section A, 1 to 30 points above it (ids of up to 16 letters, digits, `_` and `-`, unique without regard to case; optional names) and the stream network they snap to (`threshold_m2`, at least 10,000). Every point snaps to the nearest stream cell of that network within 100 m, and a point at most 50 m above a junction where its path meets another branch of the network (its tributary's mouth, the merge of its path with another point's, the confluence below a gauge) moves to that junction, so no reach of a few metres is made; the flow paths of the points form a tree on the main river of each point, whose subcatchments (the headwater, the tributaries to their mouths, the przyrzecze of every reach) are made of the 1 ha sub-catchments, split where a point lies inside one, and add up to A's catchment to the cell (500 `network_area_mismatch` otherwise). The response gives each subcatchment the parameters of a delineated watershed, each reach its length (the difference of the flow distances of its ends), its NMT slope (0.0001 where the NMT does not fall, with a notice) and its BDOT10k check length, the junctions with their inflows, the points where they snapped, A's boundary and the notices (`over_scs_cn_limit`, `reach_slope_minimum`). Its `handoff` is one `nogat-handoff/1` with a `hydrolog-network/1` of the whole network in the order each node follows the nodes it receives (subcatchments with the `curve-number` runoff of Hydrolog's defaults, Muskingum-Cunge reaches without a section, junctions, `OUT`), Polish descriptions of every node and `node_provenance`, the origin of every subcatchment, reach and junction; no rainfall, time step or result, which the person sets in Hydrolog's „Sieć i trasowanie”. Errors: 400 `invalid_request`, `network_point_outside`, `network_points_coincide`; 404 `network_point_off_stream`; 422 `network_window_too_large` (a window of more than 60 M cells); 503 `catchment_graph_unavailable`, `network_rasters_missing`, `raster_grids_differ` and `network_delineation_busy` (each worker delineates one network at a time, a request waits 15 s); 500 `network_delineation_failed`. The vendored `schemas/nogat/handoff-1.schema.json` is Nogat `8848c26` (SHA-256 `84c6856d…42ed`), with `node_provenance`.
- **Where the NMT flow leaves given polygons** (new `scripts/diagnose_catchment_exits.py`): a diagnosis of a `polygon_to_upstream_area_ratio` of the export far from 1. For every polygon of a layer (or the keys of `--ids`) the script groups the cells whose D8 flow leaves the polygon into exits (connected cells with the same destination: a polygon of the layer, a cell of none, a cell without NMT data, a pit), accumulates the flow within the polygon, so each exit gets the area of the polygon that leaves through it, and marks the exit of the export's outlet. It writes a GeoPackage (the exits as points with their destination, area, share, mean slope and NMT upstream area; the part of the polygon draining to each exit of at least `--min-exit-km2` as polygons; a summary per polygon) and the summary as CSV, reading the rasters of the run as the export does, in the window of each polygon only, without the database.
- **The bootstrap refuses NMT sheets that leave the area uncovered** (`--allow-uncovered`; ADR-076, new `utils/nmt_coverage.py`, `scripts/bootstrap.py`, `core/messages.py`): the frames of the sheets of the mosaic (their meridians and parallels in EPSG:2180) are compared with the area (the outline of `--project`, `--boundary-file` or `--bbox`, the frames of `--sheets`) and with every hole the frames enclose; an uncovered part wider than three cells is refused with `no_data` (exit 14, reason `nmt_coverage_gap`), its km² and the sheets of the grid that would cover it, e.g. „Arkusze NMT zostawiają bez danych 9.751 km² obszaru lub wnętrza ich mozaiki; brakujące arkusze (2): N-33-118-B-a-4-2, N-33-120-D-a-3-3.” (h07, set 2026, whose missing `N-33-120-D-a-3-3` lies just outside the area and drained 40 km²). A set is checked before step 1, a download run at the start of step 3 on the sheets step 2 got (a sheet GUGiK has no data for counts as missing). `--allow-uncovered` logs the text as a warning and goes on; with `dem.fill_enclosed_nodata` the enclosed holes are then interpolated (ADR-073).
- **No endorheic areas from gaps in the DEM, on request** (`dem.fill_enclosed_nodata` in `config.yaml`, default `false`; ADR-073, `core/hydrology.py`, `scripts/process_dem.py`, `scripts/process_dem_units.py`, `scripts/bootstrap.py`): the depression fill takes every cell next to nodata for an outlet, so a nodata area that the DEM data encloses (a sheet missing from the mosaic) drained the land around it, and its streams and sub-catchments had no downstream link (h07, set 2026: one missing sheet, 4.9 km², drained 40.2 km², 96.6 % of MPHP catchment 1883613). With `true` step 3 interpolates every nodata area that does not reach the frame of the raster from the data around it (GDAL's inverse distance weighting) before anything else: the fill fills it like any ground, every cell drains to the outer edge of the data, and the filled cells are DEM cells of the run (owned, routed, published, `01_dem` included). A value that is not `true` or `false` is refused; the run logs the setting, step 3 reports the filled cells, a run by units records it in its settings and `bootstrap_set.json` keeps it. The default changes nothing.
- **The data area of each instance on the welcome screen and in the guide** (new `scripts/write_instance_info.py`, new `js/instance.js`; `js/welcome.js`, `index.html`, `instrukcja/index.html`, `scripts/bootstrap.py`, `core/boundary.py`): the area is no longer the hard-coded Kaczawa text, which every instance (also Hydroni) showed. The bootstrap writes `frontend/data/instance.json` (`hydrograf-instance/1`: the area's name and km², the TERYT codes of the set's BDOT10k packages, the NMT resolution and segment, the set's project, name and closing date, the time of writing) after a run whose steps 1 to 3 passed; the name is the new `--area-name`, else the Archiwista project's name, else the boundary file's stem. `python -m scripts.write_instance_info [--area-name NAME] [--boundary-file FILE]` writes it for an instance whose data already exist. The welcome dialog shows „Obszar danych: <name> (ok. <km²> km²); NMT 5 m, zestaw danych „<set>” (zamknięty <date>)”, the guide appends the name and area to its two sentences about the data area; without the file both show a neutral text.
- **The parameters of the panel for given polygons** (new `scripts/export_catchment_parameters.py`; `core/morphometry.py`, `core/divide_flow_path.py`, `core/catchment_graph.py`): for every polygon of a GeoPackage layer (in PL-1992; ESRI:102173 is accepted as EPSG:2180) the script writes to a new GeoPackage the values of „Parametry podstawowe”, „Wskaźniki kształtu” and „Pokrycie terenu” of the panel „Zlewnia” with the polygon as the boundary, computed by the code of the panel on the rasters of the pipeline and the database: area, perimeter, elevations, mean slope, the main channel with its BDOT10k part and slope, the longest flow path with its slope and overland segment, the flow path from the divide, CN, the outlet (the cell of the largest flow accumulation in the polygon), Gravelius's and Miller's indices and the share and CN of each land cover class, without the watershed length, the mean width and the indices computed from them. Control columns compare the polygon with the NMT watershed of its outlet and flag a polygon of fewer than 100 cells or with cells outside the NMT; the units are in the table `<layer>_units`. On the boundary of a watershed of the panel it gives the panel's values (a synthetic test against the API's response builder; on the cells of five production watersheds the elevations and the lengths and slopes of the channel, the flow paths and the overland segment equal the panel's to the last digit; the differences that remain are in the scripts README). `core.morphometry.area_perimeter_indices`, `core.divide_flow_path.watershed_flow_paths` and `core.catchment_graph.contiguous_real_length_km` are the parts of the API's code that the script shares, with the API's results unchanged.
- **Control columns of the runoff in the export of catchment parameters** (ADR-075, amendment of 2026-11-09; `scripts/export_catchment_parameters.py`, `core/cn_tables.py`, `core/land_cover.py`): for a storm of P = 25 mm and 50 mm, AMC II, `q_cn_mm_p25` and `q_cn_mm_p50` give the SCS-CN runoff depth of the composite CN of the polygon and `q_weighted_mm_p25` and `q_weighted_mm_p50` the area-weighted mean of the runoff depths of its soil-cover complexes, each from its own CN (the method of weighted Q, NEH 630 ch. 10, p. 10-10), both by Hydrolog's SCS-CN equation with Ia = 0.2 S, in mm to 0.01; the table `<layer>_units` describes them in the section „Kontrola”. Their difference is the error of the composite CN for that storm, largest for small storms where the CN of the complexes differ (on h07 0.40 against 1.35 mm at 25 mm). The CN of the panel, the hydrograph and the export stays the composite CN.
- **A check of the flow directions of the BDOT10k lines** (new `scripts/check_stream_directions.py`; ADR-077): for an area, the script writes to a new GeoPackage the BDOT10k watercourse lines with the evidence that each is digitized upstream (the DEM along the line and along its whole watercourse, the order of the MPHP catchment codes, runs of lines digitized against each other), a verdict, the mouth test (a watercourse with an MPHP identifier flows towards the line of its receiver) and the doubtful cases (artificial connectors through lakes, networks detached from the main watercourses), with the nodes and the watercourses drawn downstream. A diagnostic for the review on a map: the pipeline is not changed.
- **Processing the DEM by PGW units** (`process_dem --units PGW.gpkg [--units-area AREA] [--units-scratch-dir DIR]`, `bootstrap --units PGW.gpkg [--units-scratch-dir DIR]`; ADR-071, `scripts/process_dem_units.py`, `core/unit_hydrology.py`, `core/unit_ownership.py`, `core/unit_rasters.py`, `core/unit_run_store.py`): the owners are the river and remaining-area polygons of Kartograf's PGW GeoPackage; the depressions of the whole prepared DEM are filled once, with pyflwdir's fill and D8 as in the whole-raster run, every owner takes its cells from that fill, and the accumulation, flow distance, Strahler order, streams and sub-catchments of the whole area are computed over disk-backed rasters, so a run gives the rows of `stream_network` and `stream_catchments` and the rasters of the whole-raster run (a fill per owner with a halo never agreed on its seams at 5 m). A run replaces the DEM-derived network in one transaction (it needs `--clear-existing`), writes one tiled GeoTIFF per owner and raster and a VRT mosaic per raster to `<output>/unit_runs/<token>/` (`<output>/active_run.json` names the published run), keeps its scratch (the prepared DEM, the fill and the routing rasters, 17 GB on the Hydroni grid) in `--units-scratch-dir` when given and honours `dem.stream_burning`. On the Hydroni grid (233.2 M cells, 138.3 M with data) the fill takes 161 s and a peak of 4.3 GB. The default single-raster run is unchanged.
- Migration 005: `processing_runs` (input fingerprint, configuration, status `staging`/`active`/`superseded`/`failed`, at most one `active` run) and `processing_units` (one row per owner: the keys of its catchments, `pending`/`routed`/`complete`, the halo of the window its cells were taken from, bounds of its cells and the SHA-256 manifest of its files).
- **Units grouped from catchments** (`process_dem --units PGW.gpkg --units-catchments FILE [--units-catchments-layer L] [--units-catchments-key ID_HYD_10]`, the same options of `bootstrap --units`; `core/unit_ownership.py`): each catchment (e.g. the 223 MPHP catchments of Hydroni) goes to the PGW unit it overlaps most, an owner is the union of its catchments (27 for Hydroni) and the keys of each owner's catchments are recorded in `processing_units.catchment_keys`. A file in ESRI:102173 counts as EPSG:2180.
- **Resuming an interrupted unit run** (`scripts/process_dem_units.py`, `core/unit_run_store.py`, `core/unit_hydrology.py`): each unit is checkpointed with the file of its fill and flow directions as soon as it is taken from the fill of the whole grid; the next run with the same inputs and settings resumes the latest stopped or failed run and restores its intact units, filling the grid again only when a unit is not restored (`--no-resume` begins a new run); an advisory lock keeps a second process off a run. The input fingerprint reads the text of the VRTs and the size and mtime of the other files instead of every byte of the 708 sheets. `report.json` gives the wall time and the peak RSS of each stage (on Linux the kernel's peak, reset as each stage begins).
- **The API reads the rasters of the active unit run** (`core/active_run.py`, `core/config.py`): a published unit run writes `<output>/active_run.json` (the share `/mnt/magazyn` refuses symbolic links) and the raster resolvers return its VRT mosaics; a single-raster publication removes the file and supersedes the run.
- **A second instance on the same Docker host** (`docker-compose.yml`, `.env.example`, README „Run and Deploy”): `HYDROGRAF_CONTAINER_PREFIX` names the containers (`<prefix>_db`, `<prefix>_api`, `<prefix>_nginx`, default `hydro`) and `HYDROGRAF_CACHE_PATH`, `HYDROGRAF_DATA_PATH`, `HYDROGRAF_FRONTEND_DATA_PATH` and `HYDROGRAF_FRONTEND_TILES_PATH` the host directories of `cache/`, `data/`, `frontend/data` and `frontend/tiles` (defaults the same relative paths); nginx mounts the last two over its `./frontend`, so it serves the instance's data. The database volume and the network carry the compose project's name, so `docker compose -p NAME --env-file NAME.env up -d` runs an instance that shares no name, port or directory with production. Without these variables the names and paths are those of today.
- **Burning only the main watercourses** (`dem.stream_burning` in `config.yaml`; `core/hydrology.py`, `scripts/process_dem.py`, `scripts/bootstrap.py`): the stream burning and the monotonic smoothing of step 3 take either every line of BDOT10k (`all`, the default: `OT_SWRS_L`, `OT_SWKN_L`, `OT_SWRM_L`, results unchanged) or only the main watercourses (`main`): `OT_SWRS_L` with `PRZEBIEG = 'ciek główny'` that is a river (`RODZAJ = 'rzeka'`) or carries an MPHP identifier, `OT_SWKN_L` with an MPHP identifier, no ditches (`OT_SWRM_L`, whose networks form loops that falsify the flow directions). The water bodies (`OT_PTWP_A`) are burned in both modes; `bdot_streams`, the stream layer of the map and the classification of the lakes keep every layer. In county 3001 `main` burns 294 of 8062 lines (187 of 2212 km: 90 river and 184 MPHP features of `OT_SWRS_L`, the 20 canals, none of the 7014 ditches). Another value is refused (`validation_error`, exit 2); the bootstrap logs the mode, step 3 reports it and `bootstrap_set.json` records it (`stream_burning`).
- **Legend of the delineated watershed** (`js/map.js`, `css/app.css`, guide and presentation): while a watershed is shown, the bottom left of the map has the legend „Zlewnia” with a row for each of its layers on the map — „Granica zlewni”, „Ujście”, „Ciek główny (BDOT10k)” and „Ciek główny (z NMT)” (only the kinds of segments the main channel has), „Najdłuższa droga spływu” and „Najdłuższa droga spływu od granicy zlewni” — each with a swatch drawn from the style of its layer (colour, width, opacity, dashes), redrawn on a theme change; it goes when the result is cleared. The two flow paths, both in `--warn`, are easier to tell apart: the longest one has long dashes (12/8, 2.5 px), the one from the boundary round dots (4 px, 8 apart), drawn on top where they share cells. The guide's slide 16 („Wynik na mapie i w panelu”) mentions the legend and draws the same dashes and dots; PDF printed again (31 pages, 960 × 540).
- **The longest flow path from the divide, its slope and its overland segment** in the morphometry (zgłoszenie #14; Nogat plan `2026-11-05-official-tc-inputs`, D5; ADR-070): `flow_path_length_km` (the longest flow path from the watershed divide to the outlet, the value of `hydraulic_length_km`), `flow_path_slope_m_per_m` ((elevation of its head − outlet elevation) / its length, Kirpich's S), `overland_length_km` and `overland_slope_m_per_m` (the part of that path from its head to the first cell of the DEM stream network at the delineation threshold). A value that cannot be measured, or a slope that is not positive, is `null`, never 0 or a default. The time of concentration takes them (see Changed). The panel „Zlewnia” names the lengths clearly: „Najdłuższa droga spływu (od działu wodnego)” with its slope and the two overland rows, „Długość cieku wg BDOT10k” (was „w tym ciek BDOT”, `real_channel_length_km`, unchanged) and „Droga spływu z granicy (kontrolna)” (was „Droga spływu z działu”). **Not done:** the length of the dry valley asked for in #14.
- **Flow accumulation thresholds of 1 km² and 10 km²** (zgłoszenie #27): the preprocessing makes stream networks and sub-catchments of 10000, 100000, 1000000 and 10000000 m² (`core.constants.PIPELINE_THRESHOLDS_M2`, the bootstrap and the default of `process_dem.py`); `/api/tiles/thresholds`, the catchment graph, the tiles and „Próg akumulacji spływu” take them from the database (1 ha, 10 ha, 1 km², 10 km²). Migration 004 adds their partial GIST indexes.
- Preprocessing (ADR-070): migration 004 adds `flow_path_head_elev_m`, `overland_length_m`, `overland_drop_m` and `stream_head_elev_m` to `stream_catchments`, measured by `process_dem.py` on the filled DEM. `scripts/backfill_flow_path_heads.py` fills them for existing data from the rasters their run saved; `process_dem.py --from-intermediates` adds thresholds from those rasters without running the DEM again; `generate_tiles.py --thresholds` builds the tiles of some thresholds and keeps the others. Production procedure: `docs/runbooks/2026-11-v050-reprocessing.md`.
- **Live cursor coordinates on the map** (zgłoszenie #23; new `js/coords.js`, `js/map.js`, `index.html`, `css/app.css`): the bottom left of the map shows the position of the cursor as it moves, in the system chosen in the readout: „WGS84” (degrees with 5 decimals, e.g. „51,00000° N 15,90000° E”), „PL-1992” (EPSG:2180) or „PL-2000” (EPSG:2176–2179, the zone by the longitude: 5 west of 16.5°E, 6 to 19.5°E, 7 to 22.5°E, 8 east of it, shown as „strefa 5”). The choice is remembered. The Gauss–Krüger projection is computed in the browser (Krüger's series on GRS80) and agrees with PROJ to 3 nm on 126 points across Poland. Screens without a hovering pointer (touch) do not show the readout: „Identyfikuj” gives the coordinates of a tapped point. Only the Polish systems are supported; Czech systems are out of scope for now.
- **„Identyfikuj”: PL-2000, CN for AMC I, II and III, main channel and distance to the outlet** (zgłoszenia #23, #25, #26; `core/point_info.py`, `api/endpoints/identify.py`, `models/schemas.py`, `utils/geometry.py`, `core/catchment_graph.py`, `js/identify.js`): `GET /api/identify-point` returns `coordinates.pl2000` (zone, EPSG code, easting and northing in the zone of the longitude), shown in the card as „PL-2000/5” with its „Kopiuj” beside WGS84 and PL-1992; `land_cover.cn_amc_i` and `cn_amc_iii` beside `cn` (AMC II), converted by Hydrolog's `SCSCN.adjust_cn_for_amc` as for the hydrograph and shown as „CN (AMC I / II / III)”, e.g. „61 / 78 / 89”, with the (i) of the AMC conversion (the CN of a land cover class does not depend on the soil group, as in the watershed CN); `subcatchment.channel_length_km`, the main channel at the downstream end of the segment traced as for a watershed delineated there (equal to its „Długość cieku głównego”), and `distance_to_outlet_km`, the flow distance from there to the outlet of the network, where the water leaves the data area (not necessarily the mouth of the river), both from the in-memory catchment graph (new `CatchmentGraph.trace_downstream`) and null without it. The card lists them under „Na końcu odcinka” with the catchment area.
- **Export of the hietogram and of its PMAXTP points** (zgłoszenie #39; new `js/rain-export.js`, `index.html`, `css/app.css`): below the balance of the Hietogram section „Hietogram CSV” (step, start and end [min], depth, effective depth and cumulative depth [mm]), „Hietogram JSON” (the steps with the scenario: duration, probability, total, time step, hietogram type with α and β, interpolation, and the PMAXTP points) and „Punkty PMAXTP (GeoJSON)” (WGS84 per RFC 7946: each grid point of the depth with its depth, weight, Thiessen polygon area inside the watershed or IDW distance and its PL-1992 coordinates; with the watershed boundary for a watershed mean or the interpolated point for IDW; hidden for an own series). The balance table gains the row „Opad PMAXTP” („średnia w zlewni, N pkt” or „w punkcie (IDW), N pkt”), with an (i). API: `precipitation.interpolation` and `precipitation.pmaxtp_points` of `POST /api/generate-hydrograph`.
- **Legend of the layer „NMT (wysokości)”** (zgłoszenie #20; `js/map.js`, `js/layers.js`): while the layer is on, the bottom left of the map shows „Wysokość terenu [m n.p.m.]” with the hypsometric ramp the DEM tiles are rendered with (`COLOR_STOPS` of `utils/dem_color.py`), spanning `color_min_m` to `color_max_m` of `/data/dem_tiles.json` (or `/data/dem.json`), the 5th to 95th percentile of the elevations: „≤ 160”, 337 and „≥ 513” m for the current data. - **A lake with an outflowing watercourse is exorheic** (`core/hydrology.py`, `classify_endorheic_lakes`; `--waterbody-mode auto`, step 2b of `process_dem`, both paths): the rule is the author's: a water body (OT_PTWP_A) that any watercourse line (OT_SWRS_L, OT_SWKN_L, OT_SWRM_L, whatever its kind or name) leaves is przepływowy and never gets a drain point. The old code took each line within 20 m of the lake, compared the DEM at its two ends (the end nearer the lake had to be 0.1 m higher than the other) and counted a line with no DEM data at an end (the surface of a lake is often a void in the NMT: 3,607 lines on the Hydroni area, read on the raw DEM) as an inflow, so a line that crosses a lake (both ends far from it, the nearer one arbitrary) or one that starts at a lake with a flat or void DEM left the lake endorheic. On the Hydroni BDOT10k (`hydro_merged.gpkg`, 14,013 water bodies, 137.6 km², the classification alone, the DEM read from the raw 5 m mosaic of the run) the old code left 11,096 endorheic (42.2 km²; the published run's 11,295 came from its burned DEM) and 2,917 exorheic; Jezioro Wronczyńskie Małe, touched by four main watercourses, was among the endorheic. Now a line leaves a lake when its flow starts at it and goes on outside it: the line has a part beyond its last contact with the lake (it starts at the shore, begins within 20 m of it, i.e. short of the shore as BDOT10k often leaves it, or crosses it); a line that ends in the lake, at its shore or short of it is an inflow, and so is one that only passes within 20 m. The flow of a line goes the way BDOT10k digitizes it (a watercourse is digitized downstream: 94.6% of the lines longer than 500 m whose ends differ, ADR-072), except where the DEM along it rises that way by more than 2 m on at least 200 m, a line digitized upstream, the rule of the stream smoothing (29 lines near lakes of the Hydroni area); the elevations at the ends of a line decide nothing any more. The 20 m clusters of water bodies are unchanged (an outflow of any member makes the cluster exorheic). On the same data: 9,802 endorheic (17.2 km², −1,294 lakes, −25.0 km²) and 4,211 exorheic; Wronczyńskie Małe and Wronczyńskie are exorheic. Of the endorheic ones 9,213 are touched by no line at all and 589 only by lines that end in them, lie inside them or join them to a neighbour of their cluster. The diagnostics lose `undetermined` and gain `streams_reversed`; `_sample_dem_at_point` is gone. The published Hydroni run changes only after a new run with `--waterbody-mode auto` (it ran with `none`).
- **A second import of the land cover doubled it** (`scripts/import_landcover.py`, bootstrap step 4, `prepare_area --with-landcover`; ADR-074): every import of a county package inserted its rows next to those already there, so the Hydroni table holds every polygon twice and the areas of the panel's land cover were doubled (the shares were not). - **Sink fixing never creates flow cycles** (`core/hydrology.py`, `fix_internal_sinks`, `_internal_sinks`; found in the memory analysis of `process_dem`): the `max_acc` strategy gave each "internal sink" a direction towards the neighbour of the largest accumulation, and that neighbour drained back, so the fixes made pairs of cells that drain into each other. Every one of those cells was a pyflwdir pit next to nodata, an outlet of the area and not an internal sink; Kahn's accumulation skips a cycle, so the accumulation of the outlets left out the area of the partner, and the fixes never reached `d8_fdir`/`flw`, so the flow distance, Strahler and the catchments were computed on a network that differed from the saved flow directions. An internal sink is now a cell without a D8 direction whose 8 neighbours all have data (a pit on the frame or next to nodata, an internal nodata area or a drain point of a lake stays an outlet), and the strategies choose only a neighbour whose flow does not come back to the sink (a numba path check, with the fixes made so far), so no fix can create a cycle. On pyflwdir's output nothing is fixed: `fdir` is pyflwdir's grid and `acc` equals its `upstream_area`; every cell reaches a pit and the accumulations of the pits add up to the cells of the network. **The results change:** on the 5 m raster of the v0.5.0 reprocessing (`precut`, 42,058,347 cells with data) 2574 pits next to nodata end 42,058,378 cells (1051.5 km²), where the saved run had 2577 fixed cells, 2335 pairs of cells draining into each other and the accumulation of 2326 outlets understated by 41,890,933 cells in total (1047.3 km²; one outlet by 32,526,822 cells, 813.2 km²). Rasters saved by earlier runs keep their cycles until `process_dem.py` runs again; its `--from-intermediates` reads the saved ones as before.
- **The longest flow path on the map stopped short of the outlet and cut corners** (`core/divide_flow_path.py`, `core/watershed_service.py`, `api/endpoints/watershed.py`, `api/endpoints/hydrograph.py`): `longest_flow_path_geojson` was the stored `longest_flow_path_geom` of the head sub-catchment, with the main channel appended only when that sub-catchment was on it, so on the Kamiennik at Świerzawa (37.23 km², threshold 100000) the line was 2.21 km long for `flow_path_length_km` 16.05 km; and both flow path lines were simplified to 10 m, so they cut the corners of the D8 steps and left the streams (the boundary path: 14.86 km for 16.05 km). In addition the DEM tiles were skipped on a re-bootstrap (the skip guard `tiles_missing`); they are now always regenerated.
- **504 timeout for large watersheds (ADR-042):** batched union with a pre-simplification for >100 segments, a simplified boundary for the land cover/HSG queries (18.5s → 1.6s), `_MAX_MERGE` 500→300. Response time: 95 km² = 7s (was 24s), 674 km² = 7s (was a timeout).
- **CatchmentGraph auto-reload after a bootstrap:** the API reloads the watershed graph automatically when a bootstrap from the admin panel finishes (it needed a container restart before).
- **Missing DEM sheets (38.6% NoData):** `bootstrap.py` uses `kartograf.find_sheets_for_bbox()` instead of its own `utils.sheet_finder` (91 → 192 sheets for the same bbox).
- **Tippecanoe bottleneck at the threshold 1000:** zoom Z14-18 instead of Z8-18 for the catchments of threshold 1000 (7.5 h → 6.5 min).
- `--tc-method` of `scripts/analyze_watershed.py` lists `nrcs`, Hydrolog's name of the NRCS method, instead of `scs`, which Hydrolog rejects (`Unknown method: 'scs'`); a wrong value is now refused when the arguments are parsed. The short-rainfall step reads the runoff depth of the SCS-CN result from `total_effective_mm`, a float by type (`effective_mm` may be an array; for one precipitation value both are the same number).

### Security
- **certifi 2026.7.22 → 2026.10.23** (`backend/uv.lock`): PYSEC-2026-4320, reported by `pip-audit` in CI on 2026-11-03; production image rebuilt the same day.
- **CRITICAL: a full bypass of the admin panel authorization through the query param `expected_key`** — `verify_admin_key` had a keyword-only param `expected_key` without a FastAPI marker, so FastAPI exposed it as an optional query parameter on every `/api/admin` route. A client could supply both sides of the comparison (`?expected_key=x` + header `X-Admin-Key: x`) and get HTTP 200 without knowing the real key: unauthorized remote admin access (cleanup, bootstrap/start, feedback, system metrics), exploitable from the internet through the running Cloudflare Tunnel. Fix: the dependency `verify_admin_key` takes only the header `X-Admin-Key`; the logic moved to the internal `_check_admin_key`/`_resolve_expected_key` (a test seam outside the signature that FastAPI sees); the key comparison is the constant-time `secrets.compare_digest` instead of `!=` (removes a timing side channel). The regression `TestExpectedKeyNotClientSettable` checks that `expected_key` is absent from `app.openapi()` and that the bypass attempt is refused (403 then, 401 since ADR-059). Found in the security audit of the admin panel.
- Path traversal in the upload endpoint of the sewer data (validation of the file name).
- SQL injection in load_from_database (the table name is validated against an allowlist).
