# Plan: process the Hydroni DEM by PGW catchments without merging unit-graph cycles

Approved: 2026-11-06 (the author delegated the Hydroni architecture: “masz wolną rękę”; this design implements D3 of the Nogat plan `docs/plans/2026-11-06-hydroni-instance.md`). Measurements and the correction to D3's cycle-merging proposal: 2026-11-07.

Requirements: Nogat Hydroni plan D3; Hydrograf ADR-071; the completed F1–F7 memory work. Scope is preprocessing, not a change to watershed/hydrograph API semantics. The existing single-raster bootstrap remains the default; the opt-in PGW path is `--units FILE`.

## Measurement before choosing a unit model

The throwaway experiment used all **708 active 5 m EVRF2007 ASCII sheets** in Kartograf's `pl_1992_5m_evrf2007/N-33/` store (not the duplicate `versions/` or `full_sheet/` trees), mosaicked as a GDAL VRT on a 5 m EPSG:2180 grid. `gdalwarp -r bilinear -tr 20 20` produced a **4× downsampled** DEM on the snapped window `(350140, 507000, 422360, 587280)` (14,494,554 pixels; 8,626,239 DEM-data pixels). The window extends at least 1 km beyond the `obszar` polygon. The hydrology was this branch's `process_hydrology_pyflwdir`: nodata-hole repair, pyflwdir Wang–Liu depression fill with edge outlets, D8 direction and accumulation, and its internal-sink fix. **No stream burning** or artificial cut at PGW boundaries; only the tally of D8 source and receiver cells is masked to the area polygon. The 20 m PGW ownership mask contains **7,351,753 cells = 2,940.7012 km²** (polygon: 2,940.6894 km²), with **zero unlabelled interior cells**: 26 river JCWP catchments plus `Pozostale_obszary_JCWP` `LW10235` (31.09 km²). Lake direct catchments are already inside river catchments; adding them does not partition the area further.

| Measurement, 20 m | Result |

“Downstream” in that proxy means the D8 receiving cell belongs to the river JCWP whose `MS_KOD` labels the mapped recipient line, **not** a sort of the PGW codes. The GeoPackage supplies centerlines, not an authoritative directed unit topology. Distance is estimated from the source cell center, at most 28.3 m from its D8 receiver on this grid; 250 m allows mapping/DEM misalignment. This is a deliberately conservative *classification*, not a requirement to snap the DEM to that centerline; the 93 unclassified high-area crossings must still be routed. In particular the `Rzeki_glowne` layer has one feature named Warta and is not a complete substitute for the river-JCWP lines.

All 27 units form **one strongly connected component** in the raw unit graph: simply merging each cycle merges the **whole 2,940.7 km² area**, defeating the memory objective. Even retaining only crossings ≥1 km² leaves SCCs of **11 and 9 units**; retaining only crossings ≥10 km² leaves **five** SCCs, of sizes 2, 3, 2, 2, 2. Large back-and-forth edges exist: `RW600010186589 → RW60001618699` reaches **1,135.22 km²**, and the reverse reaches **928.19 km²**. These are successive crossings of a D8 path through polygon borders, not a D8 cycle.

To estimate resolution error, crop the *same* `(382420, 563620, 416380, 581740)` window enclosing `RW6000101883689` and `RW600010188529` plus 500 m to 20 m (from the area mosaic) and 5 m (from the original VRT), and rerun the same hydrology for each window. The 5 m window has **24.61 M pixels**; these are **local-window** upstream areas, not a full-area 5 m run. For `RW600010188529 → RW6000101883689`, 20 m found **96 crossings, 19.09 km² summed, 18.864 km² maximum**; 5 m found **444 crossings, 20.26 km² summed, 20.052 km² maximum**. The largest transfer changes **+6.3%**; crossing count divided by pixel size changes from 1,920 m to 2,220 m (**+15.6%**). The reverse is 130 / 560 cells and 2.58 / 3.20 km² summed, with 1.27 / 1.28 km² maximum (20 m / 5 m). A nearby *other* pair, `RW60001618859 → RW600010188529`, had a 102.48 km² maximum at 20 m but only **0.38 km² at 5 m** in the same cropped window. Thus no 20 m area cutoff is a safe rule for merging 5 m units; plan the actual run from its own 5 m D8 topology. Boundaries, the finite window and absence of stream burning limit extrapolation of these numbers to the final production DEM.

The same 20 m DEM window has **7,876 D8 inflows from valid cells outside `obszar` into the PGW union**; the largest source accumulation is **82.352 km²** (767.834 km² summed, with repeated crossings). This is a limitation of the chosen **input raster boundary**, not a mandate to extend the domain. The units run must process exactly the cells the single-raster run of that input would process: valid cells outside the PGW union are assigned to a nearest existing owner within the input, and nodata remains nodata. Any upstream area beyond the input is truncated in *both* modes; never lose an inflow inside the input merely by clipping ownership to `obszar`.

## Decision and implementation sequence

1. **Ownership and preparation.** On one snapped EPSG:2180 5 m grid, rasterize the 26 river polygons clipped to `obszar` and `LW10235` as the 27 named PGW owners. Direct lake polygons do not create new owners. Check no interior PGW gaps or double owners at the chosen pixel-center convention; preserve PGW codes in the manifest. Do not merge `LW10235` or strongly connected river units merely because a graph has a cycle: a larger unit risks crossing the 4 GB limit. **Keep the exact input DEM domain of the default single-raster run:** outside the PGW union, assign each valid input cell to its nearest owner (or keep it nodata if the input itself is nodata), without expanding the input raster or creating fringe owners. Outside-domain inflows are truncated in both modes. The rasterized ownership, grid, input file digests, resolution, stream-burning settings and thresholds are immutable for a run.
2. **Bounded flow-direction preparation.** Process each owner with its **500 m DEM buffer** using the existing burn/fill/D8/sink-fix primitives and F1–F7 memory discipline. 500 m is a working halo, **not** a hydraulic wall or a proof of correct filling. Save the filled DEM and D8 for the *owned cells only*, including every crossing edge as `(source unit, source cell, receiving unit, receiving cell)` on the common grid. Compare directions/filled elevations in overlapping halos; resolve cross-halo depressions/spill elevations and flat tie-breaking consistently (expand a conflicting window or use disk-backed spill/boundary summaries), rerun affected tiles and refuse to publish seams that do not agree. The receiving cell owns itself and is counted **once**. Never publish duplicated buffer cells. Check D8 acyclicity at the cell/event level; a unit-graph cycle alone is normal and is **not** a reason to merge units.
3. **Accumulation and scheduling.** Build the directed **boundary-event graph** from D8 crossing edges. Within each unit, compute accumulation with the outside inflows initially zero and map each incoming boundary event along its D8 route to its next outgoing event or outlet; persist those small transfer summaries. Topologically propagate upstream cell counts through the event graph, adding each outflow's complete upstream count **once at the receiver cell**, before that cell's own contribution is sent downstream. On a local core revisit, inject incoming contributions into the local accumulator and propagate through it; write owned-cell accumulation. The event graph follows an acyclic global D8 raster even though the *quotient graph of units* is cyclic. Unit files can be prepared in deterministic PGW-code order for locality; **there is no topological order of whole units**. Run the event queue upstream→downstream, then its reverse for outlet-dependent values; spill/reopen unit files rather than load all units. An actual D8 cycle or unresolved seam is an error, not an arbitrary cutoff or a silent broken link.
4. **Finalize both directions.** Use globally corrected accumulation for each threshold's stream mask, vectorization and Strahler order. Carry upstream branch order/count through event boundaries (a seam without a confluence does not increment Strahler); similarly carry the head-cell and longest-path candidates needed for catchment statistics. In the downstream→upstream sweep, supply flow distance to the *true* outlet (including the D8 step at the seam), downstream segment identity and sub-catchment label to every upstream cell. **A seam alone is not a stream confluence or a new segment:** stitch a continuing stream and its catchment label across owners; split only at the same junction/order change as the uncut raster. Recompute per-unit stream/catchment polygons, zonal statistics, overland distances, heads and labels **after** global values are known; dissolve polygon fragments belonging to the same global catchment across a seam. Write each threshold's stream order and sub-catchment labels to owned-cell rasters. This finalization/reopening is the required **second pass**: local-only flow distance, order or labels are never published as global results.
5. **IDs, database and restart.** Count local stream fragments per threshold first and assign provisional local 1-based ids with prefix-sum offsets in sorted permanent unit-code order. Stitch seam continuations without confluences, then compact the representative fragment ids into **1-based dense global ids within each threshold**. Use this final mapping in `stream_network`, `stream_catchments`, downstream links, labels and tile attributes; preserve multi-upstream confluences as downstream links from each incoming segment, and `(threshold_m2, segment_idx)` remains the `CatchmentGraph` key. Add `processing_runs` (run id, immutable input fingerprint/config, phase/status, timestamps) and `processing_units` (run/unit key, owner code, grid extent, phase/status, output digests, per-threshold provisional counts/offsets). Make each unit's staged raster + DB write resumable and idempotent: atomic file replacement plus per-unit DB transaction; a failed unit restarts from the last committed phase, never double-counts an inflow. Stage rows by run id rather than clearing published data per unit. Only after *all* units, seam checks and global links pass, publish the new stream/catchment tables in one DB transaction and switch the associated raster/tiles manifest; leave the preceding complete run serving queries until then. `--clear-existing` applies once at publication, not per unit. Store DB metadata on local PostgreSQL disk, and unit rasters on the configured raster store.
6. **Rasters, tiles and compatibility.** Every unit raster uses the same origin, 5 m grid, CRS and nodata conventions; the 500 m halo is **not** a visible tile. Build whole-area VRT mosaics from nonoverlapping owned-cell tiles for original/filled DEM, D8, accumulation, flow distance, slope, stream order and threshold-specific catchment labels as appropriate; fail on seams, gaps and inconsistent bounds. Build existing MVT streams/catchments once from the **global** DB rows and the DEM tile pyramid from the whole-area DEM VRT, then switch tiles atomically alongside the run. Keep existing threshold URLs, SQL queries and graph lookup; the runtime windowed-read work (D4 of the Nogat plan) is required before serving a 117.6 M-cell VRT without loading it all. No `--units` means the original `process_dem`/bootstrap single-VRT path, its filenames and DB behavior remain unchanged; `--units` does not add an API query mode.

## Acceptance and memory budget

Largest observed PGW owner plus its 500 m **rectangular** buffer is about **33.8 M cells**, versus 117.6 M inside the whole area's 5 m grid. F1–F7's approximately **50 B/cell** gives **1.69 GB decimal (1.57 GiB)** of array footprint before allocator overhead, I/O caches and staged results. Run **one unit at a time**, measure resident peak on the largest unit and enforce a **4 GB** worker budget alongside production; do not increase the halo, merge units or run two workers if that breaks the budget. If a spill basin requires a larger working region, use bounded disk-backed boundary summaries / smaller owned tiles rather than silently proceeding with wrong D8 or unbounded RAM. The earlier 20 m experiment is a routing risk measurement, not proof that a 5 m bootstrap fits.

Measured on 2026-11-07: `RW600018186339` was the largest clipped owner by buffered rectangle: **8,163 × 4,139 = 33,786,657 cells**, 26,216,336 with NMT data. Reading its 5 m VRT window and calling this branch's `process_hydrology_pyflwdir` (no burn, including fill, D8 and accumulation) reached **1,545.8 MB peak RSS**, under a 4 GB virtual-memory guard. This is a **one-owner hydrology-only** measurement, not a bound on the future ownership, threshold-vectorization, DB and VRT publication stages.

Synthetic **two- and three-unit DEMs** are the reference: one whole-raster run versus the split run on the same grid must agree on filled DEM, D8, accumulation, flow distance, every threshold's stream mask/Strahler order, catchment ownership/geometry/area and downstream graph, **up to deterministic segment-id renumbering**. Include river re-entry (cyclic unit graph but acyclic D8), small hillslope leakage, two tributaries joining at a seam, flats/depressions extending beyond 500 m, outside-area inflow, and a restart after a staged unit commit. Compare stitched VRT and resulting MVT/SQL-backed graph, not just unit counts. The production run must record seam, graph, area, RSS and restart checks before activation; do not rely on the 20 m classification for actual 5 m hand-offs.

Not in this step: implementation of `--units` (this commit is the approved design and measurement), changing Kartograf's PGW acquisition or windowed runtime reads, publishing the Hydroni instance, or changing the default single-raster numerical results.
