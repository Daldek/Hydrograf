# ADR-071: PGW ownership with cell-level boundary-event routing, not cycle merging

**Date:** 2026-11-06 (measured 2026-11-07)
**Status:** accepted; implemented 2026-11-07 (opt-in `--units`); the full 5 m Hydroni run does not converge on its seams (Open, 1)

## Context

Hydroni needs the entire 2,940.7 km² area at 5 m without loading roughly 117.6 M DEM cells into one process. F1–F7 reduce per-cell memory but do not make one full-area raster safe alongside production. The Nogat Hydroni plan `docs/plans/2026-11-06-hydroni-instance.md`, D3, delegates processing by PGW units and initially proposes merging cyclic unit graphs. The approved, measured Hydrograf implementation plan is `docs/plans/2026-11-06-processing-by-units.md`.

A 20 m bilinear mosaic of all 708 NMT sheets, using this branch's real depression fill, D8, sink fix and accumulation without stream burning, has 26 river JCWP owners plus one `LW10235` remainder, covering every area cell. It contains **25,079 D8 crossings on 123 ordered unit pairs**: 24,962 cross with <1 km² upstream (hillslope scale), 24 with ≥10 km² upstream within 250 m of the receiving JCWP river line (conservative main-river proxy), and 93 intermediate or unmapped high-area crossings. Their summed source areas are 114.82, 7,533.16 and 727.61 km² respectively; these are **not disjoint contributing areas** when a river crosses boundaries repeatedly. All **27** PGW units belong to one strongly connected component of the raw unit graph; edges ≥1 km² still make SCCs of 11 and 9 units, and edges ≥10 km² leave five small cycles. A same-window 20 m/5 m check of one pair changes the largest directed transfer from 18.864 to 20.052 km² (+6.3%); a nearby reverse edge drops from 102.48 to 0.38 km². Full details and the limits of this coarse-resolution proxy are in the plan.

The same 20 m input has 7,876 directed crossings from DEM-data cells outside `obszar` into the PGW area, with a maximum 82.352 km² incoming source area. The input raster boundary truncates upstream flow in either mode: unit processing must **not** expand the single-raster DEM domain to remedy this. Assign valid cells outside the PGW union to a nearest existing owner within that same input, while existing nodata remains nodata, so no valid input cell loses its contribution.

The unit graph is a quotient of **directed cells**, not a river topology: several crossings may run in opposite directions between two polygons while no D8 cell drains back to itself. A unit topological sort does not exist, and merging all SCCs destroys the memory bound.

## Alternatives

- Merge every unit-graph cycle (original D3 proposal): **rejected**; here it collapses 27 owners into the whole area.
- Ignore hillslope crossings or merge only pairs over an area threshold: **rejected**; 24,962 small crossings still contribute, and a 20 m threshold can misclassify even 100 km² transfers at 5 m.
- Relabel every leaking hillslope or winding main-river path to its receiving PGW unit: **rejected as a default**; ownership then depends on iterative DEM divides, can swallow large areas and does not bound the next cycle or the rectangular raster footprint.
- Preserve PGW owner polygons; route the D8 boundary **events**, which form a DAG whenever the global D8 is acyclic: **chosen**. This keeps the PGW map intelligible, memory bounded by a local window, and exactly accounts for every crossing without conflating the unit graph with the flow graph.

## Decision

`--units FILE` opt-in to the bootstrap accepts the PGW GeoPackage. Use the 26 river JCWP catchments clipped to `obszar` plus `LW10235` as one separate owner; direct lake catchments already overlap river owners and do not make new units. Rasterize owners without gaps on a snapped EPSG:2180 5 m grid. Within the **same clipped/buffered input raster as the single-raster path**, assign valid cells outside these PGW polygons to the nearest existing owner, not a new fringe owner; input nodata remains nodata. For each unit use a **500 m DEM halo** for burning, filling and D8 within that input. Halo cells never become duplicated owned raster/DB outputs. Verify consistency in overlaps, propagate spill/flat boundary conditions and refuse an unresolved seam; 500 m by itself does not guarantee identical whole-raster fill/D8. Preserve genuine drain points and the single-raster sink rules. Process one owner at a time; if a larger depression or bbox breaks the 4 GB worker budget, spill boundary summaries to disk or split owned work, never merge cyclic units into an unbounded region.

**Seam proof, not a boundary outlet assumption:** run the same pyflwdir depression fill, D8 and sink rules on each buffered DEM window, then compare its filled elevations and *final D8* with every already-owned adjacent cell in the 8-neighbour seam. A cross-border depression can raise elevations on both sides; a flat can produce different D8 tie-breaking despite *identical* filled elevations (observed on the real 20 m mosaic). On disagreement, retry all owners with a larger halo within the 4 GB cap so both sides use compatible spill/flat context; otherwise fail the run, never pick whichever side ran first. The two-/three-owner reference test asserts **whole-raster equality for both fill and D8 on every cell and explicitly on seam cells**, with a filled depression and plateau crossing that seam, plus a cyclic owner graph and a low-area hillslope crossing.

The existing hole filler also temporarily repairs some **original input nodata** cells for pyflwdir routing, then restores their output DEM nodata. Preserve their transient D8/accumulation as scratch flow-through cells where local windows overlap; do **not** assign these cells a PGW polygon owner or turn them into new DEM-data/output tiles. This prevents a patched hole draining across a PGW seam from silently losing its upstream contributions. Test both the original nodata-preserving filled DEM and these cells' D8/accumulation against the uncut baseline.

Store D8 boundary events with exact `(source owner, source cell, receiving owner, receiving cell)` identities. Form local D8 transfer summaries; topologically route these **events** upstream→downstream, injecting the complete outflow accumulation into the **receiving cell** (once), then adding that cell's own contribution and routing onward. A river can revisit a PGW unit without making a cell-level cycle. Persist local cores and reopen them for the second/finalization phase: globally corrected accumulation drives stream thresholds, Strahler order and upstream path heads; downstream→upstream propagation supplies the true flow distance to the final outlet, downstream segment identity and every catchment label. Count the seam D8 step once. A seam **without a confluence or order change keeps the same global segment and label**; a single upstream continuation must not inflate Strahler order. No local-only accumulation, flow path, order, label or catchment polygon may be presented as global. Dissolve owned geometry fragments by global catchment id where they straddle a unit edge.

Allocate provisional 1-based segment-fragment ids per threshold by deterministic unit order and prefix-sum offsets of local fragment counts. Stitch seam continuations with no junction, compact their representatives into **1-based, dense global `segment_idx` per threshold**, and remap stream rows, catchment rows, label rasters and cross-unit `downstream_segment_idx`; multiple upstream segments keep their own downstream links. Preserve the existing `(threshold_m2, segment_idx)` `CatchmentGraph` lookup and CSR traversal; no API compatibility alias or separate unit-scoped graph id. Add `processing_runs` and `processing_units` tables to record immutable input fingerprints, grid/config, unit phase, output checksums and per-threshold provisional counts/offsets. Write per-unit results to run-scoped staged DB rows and owned-cell raster files with an idempotent transactional unit checkpoint. Restart reuses only units whose stored inputs/outputs still match; stale or partial outputs are recomputed. The published tables, rasters and tiles stay from the preceding complete run until validation succeeds and a single publication transaction activates all new rows and the associated raster/tiles manifest. Do not clear shared tables once per unit.

Write each raster type as nonoverlapping, one-grid owned-cell tiles (including filled DEM, D8, accumulation, flow distance, stream order and labels); expose whole-area VRT mosaics that contain **no buffer overlap** or holes. Generate existing MVT streams/catchments from the completed global DB network and DEM tiles from the whole-area DEM VRT; publish tiles with the run. Runtime D4 must window-read the VRT before deploying the whole area. The **default without `--units` remains the existing single-VRT run** (same equations, output names and API). PGW routing changes only the opt-in preprocessing and adds no API mode.

## Consequences and verification

The 500 m buffered largest owner has about **33.8 M cells**; the F1–F7 estimate is **1.69 GB decimal of arrays at 50 B/cell**, plus allocator/I/O overhead. Measure and cap one worker at **4 GB** on the production host; an event queue/streaming unit reread is preferable to merging or loading the full 117.6 M-cell area. Actual D8 seams and the peak RSS remain acceptance conditions, not assumptions inferred from the 20 m experiment.

Measured with the actual 5 m VRT: the largest clipped owner `RW600018186339` plus 500 m rectangular halo is **33,786,657 cells** (26,216,336 valid DEM cells). The project fill/D8/accumulation alone peaked at **1,545.8 MB RSS** under a 4 GB virtual-memory guard, with burning off. This measurement excludes ownership allocation, seam retries, labels, vectors, DB and tile publication.

Real-domain 20 m smoke after the no-cycle sink fix: all **27** owners, **8,626,239** originally valid DEM cells and **104** restored-nodata cells carrying D8 directions were processed without a seam conflict at the final adaptive halo. Against the uncut project run, filled DEM, D8, accumulation and 1 km² Strahler rasters were **cell-for-cell equal**; the **1,826** stream labels matched by a one-to-one id mapping, and every flow distance was within two float32 ULPs (largest absolute difference **0.0009765625 m**). The owner-core run peaked at **913.1 MB RSS** under 4 GB virtual-memory guard; this 20 m number is not evidence of a full 5 m staged run fitting the budget.

Synthetic two-/three-unit DEMs must agree with an uncut single-raster reference on filled DEM, D8, accumulation, outlet flow distance, threshold-specific stream masks and Strahler order, subcatchment labels/geometry/areas and all downstream graph links, modulo deterministic segment-id renumbering. Cases include unit-graph cycles with acyclic cells, hillslope crossings, confluences at boundaries, spill basins beyond the halo, exterior inflows and restart after a per-unit commit. Check published DB/VRT/tiles as one run; never promote a run with a missing unit, seam mismatch or unresolved cell-level cycle.

## Implementation notes (2026-11-07)

- **Global routing, not event queues.** The fill, D8 and sink fix run per owner window with seam checks; accumulation, distance, Strahler order, vectorization and labels then run once over disk-backed whole-grid rasters (a Kahn order of the D8 graph on a memmap). This is the plan's boundary-event routing taken to its limit: every crossing is a cell of one acyclic raster, no unit graph exists, and the result is the whole-raster result (the stream and sub-catchment rows are identical, ids included).
- **Burning, smoothing, buildings and lakes run on the whole DEM.** A window cannot do them (measured: 37 cells differ by up to 5.2 m where a stream enters a window), so they run once, as in `process_dem`, and the owners are processed from the prepared DEM; lake drain points are whole-grid cells that each window uses if it contains them. `dem.stream_burning` is honoured.
- **Units file.** Kartograf's PGW GeoPackage as delivered (`zlewnie_jcwp_rzecznych`, `pozostale_obszary_jcwp`); the analysis area is a separate optional file (the `--boundary-file` or project area of the bootstrap), not a layer of it.
- **Mosaics hold owned cells.** Cells without DEM data, including the repaired holes that carry flow in the whole-raster run, are nodata in the VRTs; the rows and statistics do not depend on them.
- **Publication.** One transaction replaces the DEM-derived rows and activates the run; tiles are built from the published rows by the unchanged generators, without an atomic switch. `<output>/active_run.json` (not a link: `/mnt/magazyn` refuses them) names the published run, and the API reads its VRT mosaics.
- **Owners from catchments** (2026-11-07, afternoon). With `--units-catchments` an owner is the union of the catchments (Hydroni: the 223 MPHP catchments h07) that overlap its PGW unit most, named by the unit's `MS_KOD`; `processing_units.catchment_keys` keeps their keys for the later steps.
- **Resume.** A unit is checkpointed (`routed`, with its halo and the SHA-256 of the file of its fill and D8) as soon as it is computed; a run with the same fingerprint (VRT texts, size and mtime of the other files) and settings resumes the latest staging or failed run and restores its intact units; an advisory lock refuses a second process.

Until then the full area cannot be published by units.
